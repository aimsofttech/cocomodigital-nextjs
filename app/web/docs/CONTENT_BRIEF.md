# Cocoma content brief — voice, templates, examples

For Pearl + anyone else drafting content for cocomadigital.com.
Read this once; write everything off it.

## Voice — the rules

### 1. Confident, not arrogant
Cocoma has 7+ years, 60 specialists, 70% recurring partners,
35K+ videos delivered, 400+ campaigns shipped, $600K+ generated
on YouTube in 2025. **Use the numbers** — they earn the
confidence. Don't write "leading studio" without a number to
back it.

✅ "60-person Mumbai studio. 7+ years monetizing entertainment IP."
❌ "We're one of India's leading video production studios."

### 2. The "you do X, we do Y" partnership pact
This is Cocoma's core hook. Every page hero on the work hub
follows it:
- *"You make content. We bring the money."* — IP Monetization
- *"You set the mission. We run every step of social."* — SMM
- *"You bring the brief. We make the content."* — Content Created
- *"You drop the title. We bring the audience."* — Marketing Campaigns

The pact is parallel verb-object: client owns one half (creative,
brand, IP, launch date), Cocoma owns the other (revenue, social
ops, production, audience). When you draft a hero or pitch line,
**lead with what the client brings, then what Cocoma delivers.**

### 3. Real numbers > vibes
Specific numbers earn trust. Generic adjectives don't.

✅ "70% of partnerships are recurring."
❌ "Most of our clients stick around."

✅ "$600K+ generated for partners on YouTube in 2025."
❌ "Significant revenue growth for our clients."

✅ "400+ campaigns shipped."
❌ "A long history of campaign work."

### 4. Client names + logos: OK. Attributed quotes: skip for now.
Cocoma's clients are mostly entertainment IP (BKSF Music,
Sankalp, Mx Fatafat, Psycho Saiyaan, Amazon MX Player, MX Player,
IMDb, etc.). Two different bars apply:

**Names + logos in "Trusted by" / "Built for" copy → OK by default.**
Standard industry practice in India to list client logos +
names on the website. Don't keep flagging this as a permission
issue. ✅ "Built for Amazon Prime Video, IMDb, MX Player, B4U…"

**Direct attributed quotes → skip entirely for now.**
Don't include named-and-titled testimonial quotes ("[name],
Head of Content at [Company] says…") in copy. When Cocoma
wants a specific testimonial, Anil will get explicit
permission for that exact quote case-by-case. Until then,
lean on numbers, logos, and Cocoma's own POV instead of
client mouths.

**Client-specific outcome numbers tied to a named partner → never publish.**
Hard rule, no exceptions. Applies to **all** outcome metrics, not
just revenue:
- Revenue / RPM / earnings
- View counts / watch time
- Subscriber counts / follower growth
- Growth multiples (3x, 6x, etc.)
- Show launch counts / deliverable totals
- Any number that pairs an outcome with a named client/brand/title

Even if the underlying number is publicly verifiable (e.g., YouTube
view counts on a real channel), don't pair it with the client name
in our copy. The bar is "Cocoma is the source attributing this" —
which is what we want to avoid.

If you want to show client-specific outcome numbers, **anonymize
the client every time**:

✅ "One major Bollywood music label grew channel revenue from
   $40K to $400K with our team."
✅ "A leading OTT partner: 7x channel growth, 50+ show launches,
   full CMS ops in year one."
✅ "A film studio's pre-launch slate hit 3.15M views by week one."
❌ "[Real client name] grew revenue 6x in year one."
❌ "BKSF generated $400K in 2025 through our YouTube ops."
❌ "Amazon miniTV grew from 450K to 3.15M views with our team."

Cocoma's *own* aggregate numbers are always fine — that's our
performance, not theirs:
✅ "$600K+ generated for partners on YouTube in 2025"
✅ "70% of partnerships are recurring"
✅ "35,000+ videos delivered across 60+ channels"

### 5. Founder voice (Anil's POV pieces)
First-person. "I" or "we" (referring to Cocoma + Anil + team).
Specific. Opinionated. Evidence-backed.

✅ *"Most studios that scale on quantity drop on quality. We
don't — and the proof is the 70% of partners who renew year
after year."*

❌ *"Quality is something we always strive for in our work."*

### 6. Avoid budget-agency tells
The Cocoma site reads as a premium production house, not a
budget content shop. Avoid:
- "Free trial" / "free consultation" / "no obligation"
- "Affordable" / "best price"
- "100% satisfaction guarantee"
- Counted revisions as a selling point ("up to 3 free revisions")
- Pricing in the marketing copy

✅ "Book a 15-min discovery call with Anil + a senior strategist."
❌ "Get your free creative consultation today!"

## Sticker design language (visual context for Pearl)

The site's visual language is "sticker" — 2px black borders,
4-6px hard yellow offset shadows, brand-yellow (#fff000)
accents on a black + white palette. This is the editorial
spirit too: **bold, confident, punchy** — not soft, gradient-y,
or hedge-y. Match copy energy to the visual.

## Templates per content type

### A. Founder POV essay (target: 600-1,200 words)

**Structure:**
```
Hook (1-2 sentences) — make a contrarian or specific claim
The receipt (1 paragraph) — what makes you qualified to claim it
The thesis (2-3 paragraphs) — your actual point, with examples
The implication (1-2 paragraphs) — what this means for the reader
The CTA (1 sentence) — soft, not pushy. Often "let's talk"
```

**Frontmatter template:**
```yaml
---
title: "Why our monetization compounds"
date: "2026-05-15"
author: "Anil Mahato"
image: "/Images/blog/...jpg"
excerpt: "First sentence of the essay, basically."
tags: ["founder-pov", "monetization"]
---
```

### B. SEO long-tail article (target: 1,500-2,500 words)

**Topic shape:** "How [verb] [noun] for [audience]" or "[Number]
ways to [outcome] in [year]". Match search-intent queries.

**Structure:**
```
Hook (1 paragraph) — frame the problem
Why this matters now (1 paragraph) — current relevance
The substantive how-to (5-8 sections with H2 headings)
Mistakes to avoid (1 section)
What good looks like (1 section)
The CTA (1 paragraph) — links to a relevant /service/* or /work/* page
```

**Tag with:** `["seo", "<topic>"]`

### C. Case study (target: 800-1,500 words)

**Structure:**
```
The brief (2 paragraphs) — what the client came to us for
Why they came to us (1 paragraph) — what made Cocoma the fit
What we did (3-4 paragraphs with screenshots/asset previews)
The outcome (numbered metrics: views, revenue, RPM, etc.)
What we learned (1 paragraph) — pattern that generalizes
The CTA (1 sentence) — link to relevant /work/* page
```

**Tag with:** `["case-study", "<service-line>", "<industry>"]`

### D. Hero pact line (single sentence, two clauses)

For new /work/* / /service/* / /solutions/* pages.

**Pattern:** `"You [verb the client]. We [verb the studio]."`

Examples that work:
- "You make content. We bring the money."
- "You drop the title. We bring the audience."
- "You bring the brief. We make the content."
- "You set the mission. We run every step of social."

Examples that don't:
- "Cocoma is your trusted partner for video production." (no pact, generic)
- "Together we'll build amazing content." (no specificity)
- "You give us your brand. We give you success." (vague verbs)

### E. Audience card (situation/outcome pair)

For the "Who benefits from this" section on work / solutions
pages.

**Structure:**
- **Title (the situation):** a recognizable creator-side pain
  point, phrased so the reader thinks "yep, that's me"
- **Description (the outcome):** what Cocoma delivers in
  response, phrased as "we [verb] [thing]"

Examples that work:
- *"Your IP lives across platforms with scattered revenue."*
  → *"We unify YouTube + OTT + music + sync into one
  operational pipeline."*
- *"You're posting daily but inconsistently across platforms."*
  → *"We unify your content calendar across YouTube, Instagram,
  X, TikTok, and LinkedIn."*

### F. Methodology pillar (numbered card)

For the "How we [verb]" section on work pages.

**Pattern:** Numbered (01-04). Title is "We [active verb] [noun]"
in present tense. Description is 1-2 sentences explaining what
that step actually means in practice.

Examples that work:
- "01 We figure out what your IP is worth"
- "02 We tune what's already paying you better"
- "03 We bring the brands and sync deals"
- "04 We compound the recurring revenue"

## Visual toolkit (Tier 1 — works in any markdown post today)

Long-form posts that are pure prose feel like a wall, no
matter how good the writing. Use these visual elements to
break up text and make scannable. All work as raw HTML inside
markdown — marked passes HTML through unchanged. No design
work needed; CSS already exists in
`src/components/BlogDetails/BlogDetailsContent.css`.

### Stat tile grid (4 cards across the top)

Use right after the intro to concentrate the receipts. Yellow
sticker shadow, 4-up desktop / 2-up mobile.

```html
<div class="blog-stats">
  <div class="blog-stat">
    <span class="blog-stat-value">$600K+</span>
    <span class="blog-stat-label">Generated for partners on YouTube · 2025</span>
  </div>
  <div class="blog-stat">
    <span class="blog-stat-value">35K+</span>
    <span class="blog-stat-label">Videos delivered</span>
  </div>
  <div class="blog-stat">
    <span class="blog-stat-value">7+</span>
    <span class="blog-stat-label">Years monetizing IP</span>
  </div>
  <div class="blog-stat">
    <span class="blog-stat-value">60</span>
    <span class="blog-stat-label">Specialists in-house</span>
  </div>
</div>
```

### "The implication" callout (yellow sticker box)

Use at the end of each major section so skimmers hit the
takeaway even if they skip the body.

```html
<div class="blog-callout">
  <span class="blog-callout-label">The implication</span>
  <p>The bottom-line argument in 1-2 punchy sentences.</p>
</div>
```

You can swap the label for "The receipt", "The reality", "Why
this matters", etc. — whatever fits the section.

### Pull quote (blockquote, already styled)

Standard markdown `> quote text` — renders as a sticker-style
left-yellow-border callout. Use for the single strongest line
in a section worth surfacing visually.

```markdown
> Project agencies are paid to launch. Channel operators are
> paid to compound.
```

### Image with design brief (figure + figcaption)

For visuals that need to come from Cocoma's design team. Drop
a placeholder image (e.g. `/Images/home/coming-soon.png`) and
write the design brief in the alt text + figcaption. Designer
fills the asset later by replacing the `src`.

```html
<figure>
  <img src="/Images/home/coming-soon.png" alt="DESIGN BRIEF: Line chart showing compound channel views vs viral spike. Yellow + black sticker treatment." />
  <figcaption>DESIGN BRIEF: Compound vs viral views chart — designer to fill</figcaption>
</figure>
```

### Marker-pen highlight (inline)

Wrap a phrase in `<em>` or `<mark>` for a visible yellow
highlight under the text. Use sparingly for the single most
important phrase in a section.

```markdown
The work <em>compounds</em>.
```

## Quick-fire prompts for Pearl when stuck

When drafting feels hard, pick one and write to it:

- "What do most agencies in our space get wrong about [topic]?"
- "What's a thing we believe at Cocoma that other studios don't?"
- "Walk through one specific client moment that taught us [X]."
- "Why do partners stay with us 7+ years on average?"
- "What's the difference between a campaign that compounds and
  one that spikes-and-dies?"
- "What would you tell someone considering hiring a video studio
  for the first time?"
- "What's one thing we do operationally that's invisible to
  clients but is the actual differentiator?"
